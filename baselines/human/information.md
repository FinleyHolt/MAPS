# Information sheet

**STUDY TITLE:** Measuring operator search capacity with and without an AI search assistant (Stanford IRB-90304)

**FOR QUESTIONS ABOUT THE STUDY, CONTACT:** Finley Holt, Protocol Director, frholt@stanford.edu, (716) 499-4148

**DESCRIPTION:** You are invited to participate in a research study on how many described places a
person can find on aerial imagery in one hour, either alone or by checking the answers of an
automated search system called MAPS.

You will be asked to run the study on your own computer: watch a 2-minute tutorial video, read a
short briefing, practice on four reports that do not count, and then work one 60-minute session.
Each report describes a place in California City, CA, such as a house, a pool or an intersection;
you find it on the map and click it. You have been assigned in advance to work either alone or by
checking MAPS's answers. The study software records your answers, their timing and how you used
the map (searches typed, zoom and pan). No audio, video or screen recording is made, and nothing
else on your computer is read. At the end, you email one results file to the Protocol Director.

Participation in this study is voluntary, and you are free to withdraw your consent at any time.
If you stop before sending your results file, nothing about you is kept.

**TIME INVOLVEMENT:** Your participation will take approximately 90 minutes in one sitting,
including about 15 minutes to set up the study software.

**PAYMENTS:** You will not be paid for your participation. The study costs you nothing.

**PRIVACY AND CONFIDENTIALITY:** The risks associated with this study are low: possible eye strain
from screen use. The study software is not code-signed, so your computer warns you the first time
you open it; the instructions explain how to proceed.

Your answers will be kept confidential. We have procedures to protect your privacy, and your
identity will not be included in written materials resulting from the study. Your results carry a
participant code, not your name. De-identified results, under participant codes only, may be
shared publicly with the publication.
