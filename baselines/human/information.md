# Stanford University research study: searching aerial imagery with and without an AI search assistant

Protocol director: Finley Holt, Department of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Stanford University.
Faculty sponsor: Professor Marco Pavone. IRB protocol IRB-90304.

**Purpose.** We are studying how many described places a person can find on aerial imagery in one
hour, alone or with an automated search system called MAPS.

**What you will do.** You run the study on your own computer. It takes about 90 minutes in one
sitting: about 15 minutes of setup, a 2-minute video, a 5-minute briefing, 10 minutes of practice
and a 60-minute session. Each report describes a place in California City, CA; you find it on the
map and click it. You have been assigned to work either alone or by checking MAPS's answers. At the
end, you email one results file to the study team.

**What is recorded.** Your answers, their timing, and how you used the map (searches typed, zoom and
pan). Your results carry a participant code, not your name. No audio, video or screen recording is
made, and nothing else on your computer is read.

**Risks and benefits.** Risks are minimal: possible eye strain from screen use. The study software is
not code-signed, so your computer warns you the first time you open it; the instructions explain how
to proceed. You will not benefit directly. You will not be paid, and the study costs you nothing.

**Your rights.** Taking part is voluntary. You may stop at any time without penalty by closing the
study. If you stop before sending your results file, nothing about you is kept.

**How results are used.** Results are reported in aggregate or under participant codes in scientific
publications. De-identified results may be shared publicly with the publication.
